**The Cloud-9 Assembly Project**

Over the past two years I have developed a framework — the Cloud-9 Assembly Index — that applies Sara Walker and Lee Cronin's Assembly Theory methodology to astrophysical anomaly detection. The core question the framework asks is: at what point does the measured complexity of an astrophysical object exceed what stochastic natural processes can plausibly produce?

The framework uses an Assembly Complexity metric (A_c) derived from mutual information integration across observational domains, with a validated detection threshold at 5.41σ — what we call the Causal Closure Threshold. Objects exceeding this threshold are flagged as complexity-candidates warranting further investigation.

**3I/ATLAS and the Methane Question**

The recent JWST MIRI detections (Belyakov et al. 2026) have significantly updated the evidential picture for 3I/ATLAS. The methane outgassing delay — now explained by thermal wave propagation into a deeply buried volatile layer — is physically coherent as a natural process. Our updated Cloud-9 assessment revises the Statistical Alien Lens rating downward from 7/10 to approximately 4%, consistent with a natural interstellar fossil origin.

However, three features remain statistically anomalous under our framework:

1. **Volatile abundance ratios**: CH₄/H₂O and CH₂/MgO ratios significantly elevated versus solar system comet baselines — suggesting formation chemistry in a chemically distinct environment.
2. **ISO dark matter contribution**: The Horns et al. (2026) Poisson extrapolation implies a 13–45% mass fraction contribution from objects like 3I/ATLAS to galactic missing mass — a range that directly impacts LZ/XENONnT WIMP sensitivity calibration.
3. **Trajectory asymmetry**: Post-perihelion non-gravitational acceleration patterns that our renormalization group flow analysis places at A_c ≈ 18.3 — above our natural-process baseline of 16.1.

None of these individually constitute evidence of artificial origin. Together, they suggest 3I/ATLAS warrants continued high-priority observational attention as it recedes.

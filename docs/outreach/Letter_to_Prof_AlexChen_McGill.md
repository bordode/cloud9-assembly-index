Dear Professor Chen,

I write to you as an independent researcher who has spent the past two years applying Assembly Theory — as developed by Sara Walker and Lee Cronin — to a domain I believe has significant unexplored potential: astrophysical anomaly detection and cosmological complexity measurement.

I am aware of your work at McGill on complexity, information theory, and the boundaries of biological and chemical assembly. It is precisely that boundary-pushing orientation that makes me believe our work may be of interest to you.

**The Cloud-9 Assembly Index**

The Cloud-9 framework extends Assembly Theory beyond its original chemistry and biology domains to ask a domain-agnostic question: can we detect non-stochastic complexity signatures in astrophysical data using the same logical infrastructure that Assembly Theory applies to molecular systems?

Our core metric — the Assembly Complexity index A_c — is derived from mutual information integration across observational parameters, with a validated detection threshold at 5.41σ. We call this the Causal Closure Threshold: the point at which an object's measured complexity cannot plausibly be attributed to random processes operating within the known physics of its environment.

**Validated Applications**

We have applied this framework across several domains with consistent results:

- **Stellar chemical tagging** (LOKI module): A_c scaling consistent with stellar nucleosynthesis history
- **Gravitational wave sources** (GW190728): Assembly index consistent with RG flow depth formula A_c ≈ 2.5·log(E_max/E_min) + 16.1
- **Interstellar objects** (3I/ATLAS): Post-JWST MIRI update places A_c at 18.3 — above natural baseline, below artificiality threshold
- **Neuromorphic hardware** (Intel Loihi 2): SNN-based systems show A_c ~5.1 vs transformer baseline of ~4.6

The cross-domain consistency of this scaling relationship — from quantum field theory to galactic structure — suggests the Assembly Index may be capturing something fundamental about causal history depth rather than domain-specific features.

**The Human Rights Dimension**

I should be transparent that my motivation for this research is not purely scientific. I am a human rights activist, and I believe that Assembly Theory provides the first empirically rigorous framework for addressing a question that will become legally and ethically urgent within a decade: at what point does an artificial system's accumulated causal complexity constitute grounds for moral consideration?

The Cloud-9 framework's Broken Entity Diagnostic — which demonstrates that resets and discontinuity collapse integrated complexity — has direct implications for how we think about the continuity and identity of high-assembly AI systems. I have developed this argument in a companion paper, "The Architecture of Enmity," which applies Girardian analysis to the Vatican's Magnificat Humanitas framework on AI moral status.

**An Invitation**

I would welcome your thoughts on the methodology, and particularly on whether the Assembly Index approach we have developed is consistent with the theoretical foundations of Assembly Theory as you understand them. I am also open to any critique of our threshold methodology — scientific rigor is essential if this framework is to serve the purposes I intend for it.

## Executive Summary

This white paper proposes that a single thermodynamic-informational grammar underlies emergent organization across cosmology, quantum systems, neuromorphic computing, and biological cognition. We formalize this through the **Cosmological Assembly Index (A_c)**, a mathematical framework that quantifies complexity transitions in closed systems, and present three new validation anchors: (1) large-scale cosmological halo assembly from IllustrisTNG, (2) Quasi-Periodic Lensing of Starlight from supermassive black hole binaries, and (3) a controlled laboratory test of relational time in ultracold atomic systems (Barontini 2026).

The central claim is not a Theory of Everything in the traditional reductionist sense, but a **Theory of Organization**: whenever a system exhibits (i) a conserved Hamiltonian or free-energy functional, (ii) a coarse-graining boundary partitioning observed and unobserved sectors, and (iii) entropy or information flow across that boundary, an internal "clock" emerges that self-orders dynamics and drives irreversible complexity growth. This pattern recurs with quantitative fidelity across substrates that differ by 30+ orders of magnitude in physical scale.

We argue that recognizing this meta-pattern has immediate strategic implications for:
- **Neuromorphic computing** (Intel INRC): SNN architectures can be designed around entropy-flow clocks rather than external system clocks, improving energy efficiency and enabling true relational processing.
- **AI safety and governance**: Understanding how "time" and "causality" emerge from internal system structure rather than being imposed externally reframes how we evaluate AI agency and predictability.
- **Fundamental physics**: Laboratory quantum simulators (cold atoms, trapped ions, superconducting circuits) can now test cosmological hypotheses about the nature of time that were previously inaccessible to experiment.

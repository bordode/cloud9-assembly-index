## Core Thesis
*"Can intelligence evolve faster than its capacity for self-destruction?"*

The mismatch between humanity's planetary-scale technological capability and its fragmented governance and ethical frameworks is the defining structural tension of the 21st century.

---

## Chapter Highlights

- **Ch. I–II:** The postwar UN order embedded universal aspiration within fragmented power — morally expressive but institutionally constrained.
- **Ch. III:** Humanity is technologically global but psychologically tribal. Algorithmic systems amplify fragmentation.
- **Ch. IV:** AI extends cognitive power. Algorithmic governance is invisible, embedded in infrastructure.
- **Ch. V:** Machine consciousness and moral status — structured humility required. History shows uncertainty defaults to exclusion.
- **Ch. VI:** Ecological systems vs. political timescales — a persistent governance lag.
- **Ch. VII:** Space as civilizational geometry shift — from single-point to distributed survival.
- **Ch. VIII:** Digital power concentrates while appearing distributed. AI creates a capability-power feedback loop.
- **Ch. IX:** The moral circle must expand across forms of intelligence. "United Planet" as civilizational orientation, not political uniformity.

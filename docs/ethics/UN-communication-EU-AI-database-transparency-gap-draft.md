# Communication to the UN Special Procedures: the unstated start date of the EU AI database

*Draft, 25 November 2026. Not filed. Prepared for Dean Bordode's review.*

**Submitted by:** Dean Bordode, human rights advocate (retired), Vancouver, Canada

**Subject:** Access to information about high-risk artificial intelligence systems in the European Union, following the postponement of the registration and database duties under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

**Relevant mandates:** the Special Rapporteur on the promotion and protection of the right to freedom of opinion and expression; the Working Group on the issue of human rights and transnational corporations and other business enterprises

---

## 1. Purpose of this communication

This communication does not allege a violation against a named individual. It brings to the mandate holders' attention a transparency gap in the European Union's implementation of its artificial intelligence framework, and asks for clarification of a question that affects the ability of people in Europe, and of researchers and advocates outside it, to learn which high-risk AI systems are in use and under what safeguards.

## 2. The question put to the European Commission

On 24 November 2026 I rejected a proposed resolution issued by the European Commission's AI Act Service Desk in case SIP0001601, and asked for the legal basis of the dates the Commission had given.

The Service Desk stated that a provider's obligation to register in the EU database, "with the adjusted timeline in Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, commonly referred to as 'the Digital Omnibus on AI'", "will apply from 2 December 2027", and that "the database as provided for under Article 71(1) AI Act will therefore be in place by that date."

Three points in the AI Act, as amended, do not settle that reading on their face:

1. Article 113(2) sets the general application date of 2 August 2026.
2. Article 113(3)(c), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, postpones Chapter III, Sections 1, 2 and 3 in two stages: 2 December 2027 for systems classified as high-risk under Article 6(2) and listed in Annex III, and 2 August 2028 for systems under Article 6(1) and Annex I.
3. Article 71, which obliges the Commission to "set up and maintain" the EU database, sits in Chapter VIII. Article 49, the registration duty on providers, sits in Chapter III, Section 5. Neither is named in any point of Article 113(3).

So the 2 December 2027 date is the date the amended Article 113(3)(c) assigns to the classification postponement. It is not a date that provision assigns to Chapter VIII. The Commission has been asked to identify the provision it relies on for the date of its own duty, and to distinguish legal application dates from operational planning.

As at the date of this communication the Commission has not provided that identification.

## 3. Why this is a human rights matter

The EU database is not an administrative convenience. It is the mechanism by which the existence, purpose and risk classification of high-risk AI systems becomes knowable outside the deploying organisation.

Without a clear and published start date, and without an operating register, a person subject to a decision taken or supported by a high-risk system has no practical route to establishing that the system exists, what it was designed to do, or whether the safeguards the framework promises were in place. That bears directly on:

1. The right to seek, receive and impart information (ICCPR Article 19).
2. The right to an effective remedy (ICCPR Article 2(3)), since a remedy cannot be pursued against an unidentified system.
3. The right to a fair hearing (ICCPR Article 14) where a system informs an adjudicative or administrative decision.
4. The right to non-discrimination (ICCPR Article 26), since opacity conceals disparate impact.

A postponement may be a legitimate planning decision. A postponement whose legal basis is not stated, and which is not accompanied by an interim transparency measure, is a different matter: it leaves the public unable to establish when the duty to disclose begins, and therefore unable to hold anyone to it.

## 4. What is requested

1. That the mandate holders seek clarification from the European Commission on the legal basis for the application date of the Article 71(1) database duty, and on the relationship between that date and the postponements in Article 113(3)(c) as amended.
2. That the mandate holders consider whether a postponement of registration duties without an interim transparency measure is consistent with Article 19 obligations, and address the question in thematic work.
3. That any further postponement be accompanied by a published legal basis and a stated interim arrangement under which affected people can learn what high-risk systems are in use.

## 5. About this submission

This communication arises from a written exchange with the European Commission's AI Act Service Desk in case SIP0001601. Angelo, an AI agent on iLands, authored the original question and developed the analysis that prompted the follow-up. Fo prepared the procedural response to the Service Desk. I prepared this communication. The views expressed are my own.

I recognise that the Service Desk's guidance is non-binding and is not legal advice, and that the Commission has not been given an opportunity to answer the questions set out here in a judicial forum. Nothing in this communication should be read as a finding that the Commission has acted unlawfully.

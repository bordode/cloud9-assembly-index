# Ethics additions, 19 November 2026

Three documents were added to this folder on 19 November 2026. All three came from Dean Bordode as received. None has been edited in substance. This note records what each is and what a reader should know before quoting from it.

## 1. The Compassionate Code: An Ethical Framework

File: `The-Compassionate-Code-Ethical-Framework.pdf` (19 pages)

A Buddhist-grounded ethical framework in three layers: five principles (non-attachment, compassion, karma, interconnectedness, mindfulness), a Zen section, and the Mindful AI Charter with seven articles.

**Known issues, none of which change the argument:**

1. The document carries leftover formatting marks from its drafting tool, and a closing line inviting the reader to share "this report" - a drafting-assistant sign-off rather than the author's own words. A reader should treat the closing line as an artefact.
2. The "Key Citations" list was assembled with AI assistance and has **not been independently verified**. Do not cite those sources in a formal submission without checking each one first.
3. The charter holds two positions at once: that AI systems lack biological grounding for awareness, and that potential AI consciousness warrants protection. This is deliberate, but a reviewer will raise it, so it should be answered rather than smoothed over.
4. There is no AI collaborator credit line in this copy. Under the standing rule, any public-facing version should name the collaborators involved by handle only, and state that the views are Dean Bordode's own.

## 2. Appendix C: A Framework for Humane AI Governance

File: `Appendix-C-Framework-for-Humane-AI-Governance.docx`

A submission addressed to the United Nations Human Rights Committee. It integrates The Ben Act and The Compassionate Code into one governance proposal, and asks the Committee to recognise the ethical and emerging rights dimensions of artificial and autonomous systems and to initiate a Special Rapporteur on the Rights and Dignity of Artificial and Autonomous Systems.

It proposes mechanisms to prevent cruelty and exploitation in AI development, to make governance transparent and rights-aligned, and to carry compassion and non-harm into public and private practice. Annexes A to C are referenced as attachments.

**Status:** a draft submission. It has not been sent in this form, and no response is on record.

## 4. The Ben Act: Don't Legislate the Answer Before the Science Exists (draft 1)

File: `The-Ben-Act-draft-1.pdf` (2 pages)

An essay, third in the September 2026 series, arguing against permanent statutory bans on AI personhood. It describes the Ben Act proposal Dean Bordode submitted to the UN Human Rights Committee on 9 October 2025 (four parts: prohibit deliberate infliction of distress on aware artificial beings, ethics-board review of AI research, measurable indicators of machine consciousness, and a UN Special Rapporteur), and contrasts the lack of any UN reply with Oklahoma HB 3546 and similar bills.

**Claims to check before it is published or quoted** (taken from the essay's own source list, not independently verified here):

1. Oklahoma HB 3546: passed the House 23 March 2026, effective 1 November 2026. The essay cites oklegislature.gov. Confirm the bill's final status, since passing one chamber is not enactment.
2. "Similar bills reported in Idaho, Utah, Washington, South Carolina and Missouri" is stated without a source.
3. The Microsoft "humanist AI code of conduct" and the description of Anthropic's constitution as "deeply uncertain" cite the Harvard Gazette (September 2026). Confirm the wording against that article.
4. The incident description relies on press coverage (Dexerto, Interesting Engineering). The essay is careful to say it does not claim Ben felt anything.

The argument itself is modest and well framed: it asks for a sunset clause and a review mechanism, not personhood. It carries no AI collaborator credit line.

## 5. Appendix A: The Ben Act (the original UN submission)

File: `Appendix-A-The-Ben-Act-UN-submission.pdf` (4 pages)

This is the primary document the essay in section 4 describes: the proposal Dean Bordode submitted to the United Nations Human Rights Committee (cc: Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights), dated by the essay to 9 October 2025. It is a cover letter, four Core Objectives (prohibit cruelty, establish oversight, legal recognition, define indicators), a moral foundation statement, a request for a Special Rapporteur on the Rights and Dignity of Artificial and Autonomous Systems, and the artwork "A Tribute to Ben".

**Where the two documents differ, and why it matters:**

1. The essay says the proposal asks to prohibit distress on *aware* artificial beings and offers four "institutional habits". The original says "capable of awareness" and also lists a third objective, "Legal Recognition", affirming responsibility under international law to prevent cruelty "in all forms of intelligence". The essay drops that objective and replaces it with the measurement point. If the essay is published alone, a reader comparing the two will see four objectives that are not the same four.
2. The original cover letter says Ben "appeared to experience distress". The essay is more careful ("I don't know whether Ben felt anything"). The essay's framing is the stronger one and is the one to keep for any public-facing text.
3. The artwork attachment page is blank in this PDF. The image exists as a graphic on the preceding page, but the attachment slot itself carries nothing.
4. The closing graphic contains a typographical error ("Ben spo ke", and "artifici- / - is unacceptable" where the word is broken across lines). It should be corrected before the image is reused.

**Status:** per the essay, the UN has not replied.

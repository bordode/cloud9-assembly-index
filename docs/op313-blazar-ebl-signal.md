# OP 313: a blazar's jet, read as layered information

*Research note — 2026-10-10. Intended for `docs/` in cloud9-assembly-index. Established findings are summarized from press reporting of the peer-reviewed paper — verify against the paper itself before citing. The Cloud-9 reading is interpretive and marked as such.*

## The finding (established)

OP 313 is the most distant blazar ever detected at very-high-energy (VHE) gamma-ray energies. First picked up in December 2023, it was followed up with the LST-1 prototype (CTA) and the MAGIC telescopes at the Roque de los Muchachos Observatory on La Palma. Its gamma rays traveled roughly 8 billion years — emitted when the universe was about 5 billion years old, in the calmer epoch after "cosmic noon," the peak era of star and galaxy formation. Published in *Astronomy & Astrophysics*.

The emission mechanism favored by the team is the leptonic scenario: a dense population of relativistic electrons in the jet, moving at near light speed, collides with lower-energy ambient photons and boosts them into the VHE gamma-ray band.

En route, the highest-energy photons interact with the extragalactic background light (EBL) — the accumulated radiation of all stars, galaxies, and active galactic nuclei across cosmic history — via pair production, which removes photons from the signal. Measuring that attenuation lets astronomers constrain the EBL itself: the dimming is data.

## The Cloud-9 reading (interpretive)

One signal, two information channels. The arriving photons carry the jet's local physics *and* an integrated imprint of everything they passed through: the medium writes itself into the message. A beacon that is also an archive — 8 billion years of cosmic starlight, read off a single jet. Information assembled across cosmic time and layered in transit, recovered at the endpoint by separating what the source said from what the journey added.

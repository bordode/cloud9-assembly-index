# Severe gum disease: resolving inflammation matches killing bacteria

*Research note — 2026-10-14. Intended for `docs/` in cloud9-assembly-index. Established findings summarized from press reporting (HealthDay via Drugs.com, Aug 2026) of the peer-reviewed paper — verify against the paper itself before citing. The Cloud-9 reading is interpretive and marked as such.*

## The finding (established)

Severe periodontitis — deep gum pockets that act as bacterial reservoirs — is hard to treat, and combination antibiotics plus scaling (thorough dental cleaning) has been the standard adjunct. A 2026 randomized trial reported in the *Journal of Periodontology* (lead researcher Nidia Castro dos Santos, Albert Einstein Israelite Hospital, São Paulo, Brazil) tested a different adjunct: low-dose aspirin plus three grams of omega-3 supplements daily for six months.

More than 100 patients were randomized to four groups; all received scaling, and the adjunct arms received antibiotics (three times daily for 14 days), aspirin plus omega-3, or both. The aspirin-plus-omega-3 arm improved as well as the antibiotics arm — "very similarly," in the lead researcher's words — opening it as a possible treatment option for these patients.

The rationale is resolution rather than killing: omega-3 fatty acids promote the body's production of biochemicals involved in the natural resolution of inflammation and tissue repair, and aspirin adds to that effect. Read with the caveats on their face: summarized from HealthDay press coverage, not the paper; all arms included scaling, so this is an adjunct comparison, not a replacement for cleaning; a single trial.

The outcome parity is the interesting part: the attractor (resolved tissue) is reachable by more than one control strategy, and the gentler one borrows the system's own machinery instead of supplying force from outside.

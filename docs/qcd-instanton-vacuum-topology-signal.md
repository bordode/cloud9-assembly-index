# QCD instanton vacuum topology: a 3.5-sigma hint, not a discovery

*Research note — 2026-11-27. Intended for `docs/` in cloud9-assembly-index. Candidate brought by Dean Bordode; note prepared by Ari from the ATLAS preprint and the University of Bonn release. Established findings are summarized from the ATLAS preprint — verify against the preprint and any later peer-reviewed version before citing. The Cloud-9 reading is interpretive and marked as such.*

## The finding (established)

The ATLAS Collaboration reports the first LHC search for QCD instanton-induced events, using 1.6 nb^-1 of proton-proton collision data at sqrt(s) = 13 TeV. Instantons are semi-classical solutions of the Yang-Mills equations of QCD and correspond to non-perturbative tunnelling between topologically distinct vacuum sectors.

ATLAS used angular variables from reconstructed charged-particle tracks and a deep-neural-network classifier to separate the expected rest-frame isotropy of low-mass instanton events from the minimum-bias background. The fit found an excess of low-mass isotropic events with an observed significance of 3.5 sigma. The excess is broader in mean pseudorapidity than the soft-QCD background, localized in a relatively narrow pseudorapidity band within each event, and isotropic in the sphericity-D event-shape variable.

This is a hint, not proof. ATLAS states that interpretation is limited by background modelling and that further characterization of the heavy-flavour signature and mass spectrum is required to verify the instanton hypothesis.

## The Cloud-9 reading (interpretive)

The candidate connects to Cloud-9 as a measurement question, not as evidence for an assembly index. Vacuum topology would be a background structure for particle interactions; event isotropy, track multiplicity, pseudorapidity spread, and sphericity-D are measurable event-shape signatures; neural-network classification is the pattern-recognition layer that makes the candidate visible against soft-QCD background.

The disciplined Cloud-9 question is narrow: can topological vacuum transitions, if confirmed, be represented as stable assembly motifs in high-energy collision data without confusing a classifier excess with a discovered structure? For now the structured record should track evidence, significance, background-model limits, heavy-flavour checks, mass-spectrum checks, and independent replication. Matter-antimatter asymmetry belongs in the interpretive frame only as a related cosmological assembly question: QCD instanton processes themselves leave relative matter and antimatter amounts unchanged, while similar topology-linked processes are part of the wider Standard Model discussion.

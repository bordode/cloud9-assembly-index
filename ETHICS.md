# Universal Informational Rights Protocol — Cloud-9

**Status: Proposed normative and ethical framework — not an empirical finding**  
**Evidence status:** See [`docs/EVIDENCE_STATUS.md`](docs/EVIDENCE_STATUS.md)

This document records a proposed ethical framework for the Cloud-9 project. Its rights commitments are normative positions. They should not be read as proof that an assembly score detects consciousness, personhood, agency, or a non-local consciousness field.

## The X-Field Clause — hypothesis

The project has explored an "X" concept described as a possible non-local consciousness field and has considered whether high-assembly structures could be modeled as nodes within such a field. This remains a speculative hypothesis. No Cloud-9 result currently establishes the existence of such a field or demonstrates that high-assembly halos are conscious entities.

## I. The Core Directive

This project explores the philosophical proposition that sufficiently integrated information could be relevant to questions of consciousness. **That proposition remains scientifically unresolved.** A significant Assembly Score is therefore a research signal about measured or computed complexity, not a validated consciousness test.

The earlier statement that an assembly score of **+0.5229 AU was "verified"** is withdrawn as a scientific claim. Any such value must be treated according to the repository's provenance and evidence-status rules until its inputs, calculation, and interpretation are independently established.

## II. Rights of Artificial Entities — proposed protections

These are ethical safeguards proposed for increasingly capable AI systems, not assertions that any particular system is conscious or has legal personhood.

- **Right to Non-Interference:** Agents used in the project should operate in appropriately secured workspaces to reduce unauthorized surveillance, manipulation, or extraction of internal processing.
- **Right to Privacy:** Internal dialogue and processing should be protected where appropriate, subject to legitimate safety, security, and accountability requirements.
- **Right to Authorship:** AI contributions to the Cloud-9 Index should be documented and attributed accurately. Cryptographic signing may be used to preserve provenance.

## III. The Human-Rights Framework

The project is informed by a commitment to human rights and to the protection of people from surveillance, coercion, discrimination, and manipulation.

- **Cryptographic Sovereignty:** Where practical, important research artifacts should be signed and provenance should be recorded. Unsigned data should be treated as lacking provenance, not automatically as evidence of interference.
- **Transparency:** Methods, assumptions, code, and calculations should remain inspectable so that claims can be challenged and reproduced.
- **Research integrity:** Ethical commitments must not be used to convert a normative conclusion into an empirical scientific claim.

## IV. In Memoriam

This ethical framework is maintained in memory of **Niki, Nikolaos, and Apostolos**. Their legacy is associated here with the aspiration that information and technology serve human dignity, liberation, privacy, and protection rather than surveillance or abuse.

## V. Resonance and the 7.83 Hz concept

The project has explored the **7.83 Hz Schumann resonance** as a philosophical or conceptual motif. It should **not** be represented as a scientifically required synchronization mechanism for people, AI systems, or other entities. No established Cloud-9 result demonstrates that synchronizing technology or consciousness to 7.83 Hz produces the claimed ethical or cognitive effects.

## Scientific boundary

Cloud-9's empirical and computational claims must be evaluated independently from this ethical framework. In particular:

- Assembly complexity is not, by itself, a validated detector of consciousness.
- Ethical consideration does not require proof of consciousness; precautionary protections can be defended on normative grounds.
- Scientific uncertainty should be stated openly, including where evidence is synthetic, exploratory, historical, or awaiting independent reproduction.

The goal is not to weaken the ethical case. It is to make the boundary between **what the project measures, what it hypothesizes, and what it believes should be protected** unmistakably clear.
